Strategies I ran at Lucidly Labs, 2024–26

Yashish Khurana · @0xYashish · rebuilt from scratch so you can rerun them

These are the DeFi yield strategies I designed and ran with real money for two years: stablecoin loops, funding carry, LP with a hedge, lending. The production code belongs to Lucidly, so each one here is a rewrite: the allocation and risk rules in plain Python, a replay on bundled data, and a page of results. Where the data is synthetic the page says so.

If you only read one thing, read the funding carry page. I replayed my own production close rule on 90 days of real Hyperliquid funding and it lost 34%, almost all of it in fees from closing and reopening ~400 times. The rule I shipped was bad. The fix was to trade less.

The strategies

Delta-neutral funding carry — Hyperliquid, 4 coins, long spot / short perp at 3x.
Real data, Jul–Oct 2026. The single-print close rule paid ~45 bps a round trip, ~400 times, to collect ~9%/yr of funding. A 24-hour average instead of a single print takes it to roughly flat. No funding gate at all is the best row (+1.3%, 4 closes). Gross opportunity in the window was only ~7%/yr, so this is a postmortem, not an edge.
Leveraged stablecoin loops — Morpho Blue, 3 markets, 1x to 8x.
Real data, 93 days of archive reads. Return falls with leverage once you charge gas and entry fees: +0.9% at 1x, −1.4% at 8x over the window. The interesting part is the gate: lever on net yield after borrow, not the raw spread, and keep hysteresis on the exit or you churn.
USDC lending cluster, plus the apyUSD postmortem — Morpho Blue and Royco.
Real data, same 93 days. The allocator is "mean-variance" on paper but with a 4-day window the variance term does nothing; the caps and hurdles are what bind, and half the book sat idle. The junior tranche lost 34% on an 8.5% depeg: first-loss leverage I understood on paper and underweighted in practice.
WBTC/WETH concentrated liquidity with a hedge — Uniswap v3 and Aave, Arbitrum.
One real day, 9,940 swaps, fees measured from the pool's events: ~5× what my flat fee model assumed, enough to pay for the hedge that day. On synthetic 30-day paths the hedge cuts vol but not the drawdowns, which come from IL. One day is one day.
EURC/USDC concentrated liquidity — Aerodrome, Base.
Measured fees on the window of my earlier public backtest are 23 EURC against the 75 that backtest claimed. The old one gave the position a bigger share of each swap than the pool's liquidity allows. That was my mistake and the page says so.
BTC and ETH carry vaults — Morpho Blue.
Synthetic price paths, real borrow rates. An LTV ladder (0.60 / 0.65 / 0.70 / 0.74, liquidation at 0.86) with a staged delever route. A 35% gap in a day liquidates it from 0.65; the ladder handles drift, sizing handles gaps.

There is also a small vol-cone tool I used to size margin reserves.

What I'd tell someone running these

Costs beat signals. In every one of these the deadband, the sustain window or the rotation threshold moved the result more than the signal did. Size for the same-block move you can't react to, not the one you can. And write down what broke: a drained gas wallet, a decoder missing a selector, a redemption into a term trough. Each of those became a check in the next version.

Running it

pip install -r requirements.txt
pytest            # 28 tests
python build_all.py

Each strategy is strategies/<name>/strategy.py (the rules) and backtest.py (the replay, which writes the page). Nothing under 30 days is annualised, nothing is called good without a benchmark, and none of it is a return claim.
